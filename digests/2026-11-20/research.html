AI Labs Research Digest

Week of Nov 16, 2026 (Nov 13 – Nov 20)

Here's what the major AI labs published this week.

Anthropic

Claude solves two open problems in the physics of black holes

Nov 17, 2026 · Science

Anthropic reports that Claude solved two open problems in theoretical physics. Both concern black holes and the search for a theory of quantum gravity. It adds to a fast-growing set of examples where frontier models contribute original results to research mathematics and physics, not just help with the busywork.

OpenAI

Research acceleration: The jagged frontier

Nov 18, 2026 · Research

OpenAI measures how much coding agents are already speeding up its own AI research, and where their performance is still uneven. It also discusses what those measurements suggest about the prospects for recursive self-improvement, a key question for frontier-risk forecasting.

Advancing chip design with OpenAI models

Nov 18, 2026 · Research

GPT-6 Astra optimized circuits that went into OpenAI's Jalapeño silicon and improved RISC-V core designs. It's a concrete case of AI-assisted hardware design reaching real production chips.

Can AI help scientists identify promising drug candidates? (MedChemBench)

Nov 20, 2026 · Research

MedChemBench is a new benchmark for early-stage drug discovery. It tests whether models can interpret molecular structures, reason from experimental evidence and pick the most promising compounds. It gives medicinal chemistry a targeted evaluation instead of relying on general science quizzes.

A step toward defining the Standard Model on a lattice

Nov 15, 2026 · Science

This post presents a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model of particle physics and describes how GPT-6 Astra helped turn the idea into a detailed proof. It is one of the first posts on OpenAI's new science blog, which launched the same day.

Our first phase of embedded assessments

Nov 16, 2026 · Safety

OpenAI is embedding external evaluators (Apollo Research, METR, Transluce and Goodfire) to independently assess its models' alignment, safeguards and monitoring systems. It is a step toward ongoing third-party scrutiny that goes beyond one-off, pre-release testing.

Google DeepMind

Genie 4 is now available

Nov 18, 2026 · World models

DeepMind released Genie 4, the newest version of its world-model line, which generates interactive, explorable environments. Genie is DeepMind's main research bet on simulated worlds for training and evaluating agents. This release makes the model broadly available rather than only a research preview.

Google Research

A game you cannot win unless you have a quantum computer

Nov 19, 2026 · Quantum

Google Quantum AI describes a game that only a player with a quantum computer can reliably win, giving a concrete and checkable demonstration of quantum advantage. Tasks like this make claims about quantum computation easier to verify.

Advancing science & engineering with Antigravity's /teamwork

Nov 13, 2026 · Agents

Google Research presents /teamwork, a feature in the Antigravity agent platform that coordinates multiple agents on science and engineering tasks. It shows how Google is applying multi-agent collaboration to research workflows.

Meta AI

Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding

Nov 12, 2026 (one day before this week's window, not covered previously) · NLP / Computer Vision

This Meta publication, by authors including Saining Xie, Luke Zettlemoyer, Lucas Beyer and Mike Lewis, revisits how vision encoding is structured inside multimodal language models. It focuses on the standard design in which a single pretrained vision encoder does all the work. Only the listing was read for this digest, so see the paper for its specific findings.

Other Labs

AURA: AUdio-dRiven streaming Avatar

Nov 19, 2026 · Kyutai

Kyutai's AURA generates talking-head video chunk by chunk with a rolling cache, so it needs only the current audio rather than seconds of look-ahead. The 1.3B model streams 576p video in real time on a single GPU. That makes low-latency avatars for voice assistants practical.

Aligning AI Teams

Nov 13, 2026 · Srinivasan, Matthews, Sohl-Dickstein, Jones

This paper investigates why alignment training on individual LLM agents often fails to carry over when the agents work as a team, and tests interventions to fix that. The authors find that the single-versus-team safety gap does not shrink reliably as models get more capable. They also find that intuitive fixes, such as having agents critique each other, often fall short.

Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights

Nov 16, 2026 · Fan et al.

A 27B model is given access to its own weights, graded answers and the ability to run itself. It then diagnoses its failures and edits its weights directly, with no gradients or training, and ends up beating its original performance. The authors call this "self-rewriting", a new and unusual path to self-improvement.

Other Labs entries came from arXiv and Hugging Face Daily Papers, and affiliations are listed only where the source states them.
