Hyperdimensional (Dean Ball)

The Silent Consensus — Nov 18, 2026

Adapted from a Harvard lecture given amid the AI-discovered "Black October" vulnerability disclosures, Ball says the tech world has plenty of technological visions but no positive institutional one. What fills the gap is a "silent consensus": market competition will gradually push firms and governments to hand control to more capable AI, so humans forfeit primacy without any machine rebellion. Ball rejects it outright. He argues human primacy rests on our past and our stewardship of civilization, not on a monopoly on "genuine understanding." He doubts that intelligence translates so directly into power, but concedes that fully autonomous corporations could out-compete human enterprise, and says the fight over that future has to happen in the open.

Interconnects (Nathan Lambert)

Open-source AI without Jensen — Nov 14, 2026

Lambert calls Jensen Huang's resignation a major setback for the US open-model ecosystem. Huang was its biggest funder and its political protector, and Nvidia will now focus on stabilizing itself. The open-model economy has found a real lane in "ownership of intelligence" and enterprise post-training, but compute scarcity keeps open models far from being commoditized. Lambert worries about sloppy state and federal regulation driven by "open-weight hawks," especially once a Chinese open-weight model is tied to a major hack. Quoting Dylan Patel that the bottleneck is talent, not money, he argues the only lasting fix is a truly frontier US open-model lab. Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Small is promising but still trails Kimi K3.1 Pro.

The AI Policy Newsletter: October–November 2026 — Nov 16, 2026

A bimonthly policy roundup. Understanding AI (Timothy B. Lee)

I spoke to seven physics experts grappling with the AI revolution — Nov 17, 2026

Suvrat Raju's TheoPhysEval benchmark of unpublished, day-long theoretical physics problems was saturated before release. GPT-6 Astra solved 49 of 50, at under $3 each, and found errors in the human answer key. Physicists such as Andreas Karch and Matthew Schwartz now run agents around the clock, Witten and Maldacena credit AI in their papers, and hep-th arXiv submissions are up about 30% this year. Others, like MIT's Daniel Harlow, note that most real work is "figuring out what to do." The bigger worry is for young physicists: calculation used to be how they built intuition and reputation, and Raju has stopped taking new PhD students until their prospects are clearer.

WIRED AI Lab (Will Knight)

Jensen Huang's affair is the latest twist in America's AI chip war — Nov 18, 2026

Louise Matsakis, filling in for Knight, explains the administration's new rules requiring licenses for overseas data centers that rent large amounts of compute to China, plus tighter chip shipments to Singapore, Malaysia and three other countries. She ties the timing to Huang's affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, a firm suspected of smuggling. She recounts four messy years of export controls: an underfunded BIS, about 50,000 unaccounted-for Megaspeed chips, IFP estimates of smuggled compute worth around 1M H100s, and a NY Fed estimate of $130B in lost market value for US suppliers. She warns that Washington is now trying to police how Chinese firms use US chips anywhere in the world. Also linked: Nvidia's first post-Jensen all-hands, and Thomas Nagel on AI consciousness.
