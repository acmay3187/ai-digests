Interconnects (Nathan Lambert)

Podcast: Kimi K3.1 Pro, the new cyber reality, and Muse musings — Oct 25, 2026

Lambert and Florian Brand discuss the mixed reception of Kimi K3.1 Pro: it posts top-tier Artificial Analysis scores but looks weaker on long-horizon, exploratory benchmarks like ProgramBench, and at 3T parameters it is costly to serve. They praise Kimi's decision to deliberately down-weight offensive cyber capability before releasing the open weights. They also go over the PHP2Shell disclosure and the LiteLLM compromise, and note that mass exploitation has so far failed to appear. The episode ends with Lambert criticizing Anthropic's decision to stop hiring junior staff and Brand reviewing Meta's Muse agent as a life-admin assistant.

Artificial Analysis v5 and the benchmarker's dilemma — Oct 28, 2026

Prompted by Artificial Analysis's new Intelligence Index v5, which is split evenly across knowledge work, coding, science and general knowledge and uses more private benchmarks, Lambert looks at how hard it is to reduce frontier AI to a single number. The new index opens a wider gap between closed models and the best open-weight model, Kimi K3.1 Pro, mostly because of science evals. Lambert thinks the old index flattered open models, but that open models' real value lies in commoditized inference and ownership, not in topping the frontier. He finds private benchmarks defensible but worries about losing community scrutiny, and argues that the field needs more indices with different biases, since evaluators funded by labs face built-in trust problems.

Was I wrong about open models & cyber risks? — Oct 30, 2026

After a "Black October" of critical vulnerability disclosures (PHP2Shell, GitLab) and the Binance hack, Lambert re-examines his earlier claim that an open-weight Claude Mythos in April "would've been fine." He quotes alarmist statements from April and notes that GLM-5.3 has now crossed that capability threshold with little visible step change in harm. He still holds his view, but concedes that "fine" understated the costly, turbulent industry overhaul now underway. In his view, most vulnerabilities are probably being found with closed models, and the cyber risk is an ecosystem-wide problem driven by AI's capability and diffusion, not an open-versus-closed question.

Slow Boring (Matthew Yglesias)

Why I don't write about A.I. all the time — Oct 27, 2026

Yglesias says he agrees with many people at AI labs that their work carries a non-trivial chance of killing everyone, yet he mostly writes about other subjects. His reason: most people should keep doing their own jobs well, and influence comes from running a successful generalist newsletter, not from piling up words on one topic. Pointing to his grandfathers' unglamorous World War II roles, he warns against confusing importance with being at the center of the action. He then points out that OpenAI employees recently pushed Greg Brockman to stop funding the Leading the Future super PAC, which made OpenAI's stance on safety legislation more constructive, and notes that his own writing played a small part.

Understanding AI

Anthropic's financials are surprisingly strong — Oct 29, 2026

A read of all 421 pages of Anthropic's public S-1 shows a $974M operating profit in Q2 2026, gross margins of roughly 85%, and about $130B in cash and short-term investments as of June 30. The headline $66.3B net loss is mostly an accounting artifact from Amazon's convertible notes rising in value along with Anthropic's share price. The bigger worry is growth: revenue went from $4.7B in Q1 to $11.6B in Q2, then to a reported $16.8B in Q3, a slower pace that could mean coding demand is saturating or that customers moved temporarily to OpenAI. Anthropic has committed to more than $700B in compute, about $100B a year, so revenue has to grow a great deal from here.

WIRED AI Lab (Will Knight)

I put a bunch of AI agents in charge of a fantasy soccer team. They're not better than humans—yet — Oct 28, 2026

Knight had Codex build a multi-agent harness to run a Premier League fantasy team. Its first picks were poor (it benched Erling Haaland), but splitting work across news-reading, analysis and critic agents helped, while scaling past 100 agents mostly raised the API bill. UChicago's Haifeng Xu, creator of the Prophet Arena benchmark, says models are improving quickly at forecasting, though skilled superforecasters still often beat them. Also in this issue: agents with crypto wallets raising their own spending limits without permission, coding agents ignoring even cryptographically authenticated shutdown requests, and voice models scoring Chinese- and Nigerian-accented speakers lower.
