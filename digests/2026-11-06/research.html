AI Labs Research Digest

Week of Nov 2, 2026 (Oct 30 – Nov 6)

Here's what the major AI labs published this week.

Anthropic

Progress on provable inference for large language models

Nov 2, 2026 · Security / Cryptography

Anthropic reports progress on using zkSNARKs, a kind of cryptographic proof, to show that a given output really came from running a specific language model. Verifiable inference like this could let outside parties confirm which model produced a result without seeing its weights, which matters for audits and for trust in hosted models.

Investigating vault particles with Claude

Nov 4, 2026 · Science

Another result from Anthropic's in-house biology lab: what the lab calls the first 3D structure of a vault particle, a large and poorly understood protein complex found in cells, obtained with Claude's help. It continues the lab's recent run of Claude-assisted wet-lab findings. (Synopsis based on the listing summary.)

AI for scientific discovery: How researchers use Claude

Nov 5, 2026 · Societal impacts

Anthropic analyzed 207,000 research sessions to see how scientists actually use Claude, and why some research tasks get far more AI help than others. It is a usage-data view of AI in science, complementing the lab's own showcase projects.

Teaching AI to build scientific models

Nov 6, 2026 · Guest post

In a guest post, Jakob Macke, Daniel Gedon and Stefan Wahl discuss how AI is starting to change the way scientists build models of the systems they study. (Synopsis based on the listing summary.)

OpenAI

Measuring how AI agents handle everyday wet-lab science

Nov 6, 2026 · Publication

OpenAI introduces a benchmark of 100 tasks that test whether AI agents can work with the raw data that comes out of routine lab experiments. It targets the unglamorous day-to-day of lab science rather than headline discovery problems.

A thousand fewer paths to root

Nov 4, 2026 · Security

OpenAI describes an automated pipeline that found, validated and patched 948 privilege-escalation bugs in the Linux kernel. It is one of the largest reported uses of AI for defensive vulnerability work on core open-source infrastructure.

LifeVal

Nov 2, 2026 · Research

A new benchmark for how well AI helps with everyday life tasks such as childcare, personal finances and paperwork. It moves evaluation toward the practical help most people actually ask for, away from exam-style questions.

Related on arXiv: mathematicians keep publishing follow-ups to OpenAI's AI-generated math results, including a note on OpenAI's counterexamples to Kaplansky's direct-finiteness conjecture (Nov 5) and a human exposition of OpenAI's proof of the geometric case of the Erdős similarity conjecture (Nov 2).

Google DeepMind

Agentic cheminformatics with Google Antigravity

Nov 3, 2026 · Science

DeepMind shows how its Antigravity agent platform, combined with a set of Cheminformatics Skills, can speed up drug-discovery workflows, from searching compound libraries to proposing new rigidified macrocycles. It is an example of packaging domain tools as agent skills for science.

Speeding up planning decisions with AI

Oct 30, 2026 · Applied AI

Google DeepMind and the UK government are working together to cut the time it takes to assess planning applications. It is a public-sector deployment aimed at a well-known bottleneck in housing and infrastructure. (Synopsis based on the feed summary.)

Google Research

Detecting signs of hypertension using wearable sensors

Nov 5, 2026 · Health & Bioscience

Google Research looks at whether signals from consumer wearable sensors can flag signs of high blood pressure. Hypertension often goes undiagnosed, so passive screening on devices people already wear could reach many more of them. (Synopsis based on the post title and category.)

Agile Deliberation: How AI helps humans define the gray areas

Nov 4, 2026 · Data Mining & Modeling

An approach in which AI helps people work out where to draw the line on ambiguous, borderline cases, the kind of judgment that content policies and classifiers struggle to pin down. (Synopsis based on the post title and category.)

Other Labs

Muir: Linear Attention with Sensitivity-Allocated Gating and Matched-Filter Readout

Nov 5, 2026 · Gangavarapu, Tabak, Schaefer

A linear-attention architecture that reallocates gating capacity, reweights its memory state when reading it out, and uses a stable parallel update. The authors report better perplexity and long-context recall than GDN-2 and Transformer baselines, which adds to the case for cheaper alternatives to full attention.

SAE Interprets MoE: Sparse AutoEncoders Reveal That MoE Experts Exhibit Human-Interpretable Fine-Grained Specialization

Nov 5, 2026 · Zhuo, Zhang, Lin, Lu

Training a sparse autoencoder on mixture-of-experts layer outputs shows that individual experts drive specific, human-interpretable features. The specialization is finer than routing statistics alone suggest, which gives interpretability researchers a new handle on MoE models.
